STATUS & PURPOSE:
  This analysis quantifies the empirical between-layout and calibration-day variance floor
  for hardware detection of any thermal effect. It is NOT an effect estimate.
  Observed swings reflect differing transpile layouts and drifting machine calibrations.

3. FINDINGS & SCIENTIFIC TAKEAWAYS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. EMPIRICAL VARIANCE FLOOR:
   Between 9 Oct and 12 Oct, identical benchmarks mapped with plain ESP exhibited large swings:
   - GHZ-5:  +2.64 pts (90.28% -> 92.92%)
   - BV-5:   +8.81 pts (86.32% -> 95.12%)
   - QFT-5:  +10.99 pts (74.07% -> 85.05%)
   - Mirror: +1.73 pts (73.91% -> 75.64%)
2. DWARFED THERMAL PENALTIES:
   The maximum theoretical degradation that the thermal term could penalize on the committed
   9 Oct layouts is tiny (0.90% for GHZ/BV, 2.59% for QFT, 3.33% for Mirror).
   The observed between-layout/day variations (up to +11.0 pts) exceed the thermal term penalty
   by 3x to 12x.
3. METHODOLOGICAL CONCLUSION:
   These observations demonstrate that on live superconducting hardware, background calibration-day
   drift and compiler layout choices produce a variance floor of 2 to 11 percentage points.
   Consequently, subtle thermal term effects (< 1 pt) are completely submerged within this floor.
